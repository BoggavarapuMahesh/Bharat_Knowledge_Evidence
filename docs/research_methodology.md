# Bharat Knowledge Evidence

## Research Methodology

### 1. Primary Evidence

Whenever available, use:

- Original texts
- Manuscripts
- Inscriptions
- Archaeological evidence
- Historical artefacts
- Critical editions

### 2. Scholarly Evidence

Use:

- Peer-reviewed research
- Academic books
- University publications
- Critical editions
- Scholarly translations

### 3. Institutional Evidence

Use:

- Museums
- Libraries
- Government institutions
- Universities
- UNESCO and similar international institutions

### 4. Evidence Classification

Each record should distinguish:

- Direct textual evidence
- Archaeological evidence
- Material evidence
- Inscriptional evidence
- Scholarly interpretation
- Traditional interpretation
- Modern interpretation
- Modern comparison
- Disputed claim
- Uncertain claim

### 5. No Predetermined Conclusions

The project should not begin with the assumption that every traditional
claim is either true or false.

The available evidence determines the classification.

### 6. Reproducibility

A researcher should be able to follow:

Claim
→ Source
→ Passage/Evidence
→ Interpretation
→ Evidence classification

### 7. Source Transparency

Every important claim should have a traceable source.

### 8. Historical Dating

Dates should be recorded as estimates or ranges when exact dating
cannot be established.

### 9. Modern Comparisons

A similarity between an ancient concept and a modern concept does not
automatically establish historical influence or invention.

Historical transmission must have evidence.
